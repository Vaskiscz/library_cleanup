What the app does, and who decides

Selects reads your photo library and suggests which shots are worth keeping and which can go. It suggests. You decide. Nothing is removed without your confirmation, and removal is handed to iOS’s own delete, so photos go to your Recently Deleted album and stay recoverable for 30 days.

Two features also add to your library. Compressing a video saves a smaller copy beside the original, and the original stays until you choose to remove it, which is its own confirmed step. Keeping one frame of a burst saves that frame as a new photo and then removes the burst, because iOS does not let an app remove single frames from a burst. The new photo is not in the albums the burst was in.

It will sometimes be wrong

This is the honest part. Selects makes judgments about which photos are worth keeping, and judgments can be wrong. It may suggest clearing a shot you wanted, or keep one you had no use for. That is why nothing happens without your confirmation, and why everything goes through Recently Deleted.

The same goes for compression. A compressed copy is smaller because it keeps less detail, and you see a preview first so you can judge that yourself. Once you remove the original and it leaves Recently Deleted, the full‑quality version is gone for good.

Your photo library is yours to look after. Keeping a backup is sensible whatever app you use, and it is sensible here too. Selects is provided as it is, without a promise that every suggestion will suit you.
