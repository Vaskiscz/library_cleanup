A fair question

Why isn't it on the Mac App Store?

To do its job, Library Cleanup has to read your whole Photos library and delete photos through Apple's PhotoKit. That needs Full Disk Access and delete permissions the App Store sandbox won't hand out.

Rather than gut what makes it useful to fit the sandbox, I distribute it directly.

And because it asks for that much access, the full source is public, so you (or anyone) can audit exactly what it does on GitHub before trusting it with your library.

Installing

Two permissions, then you're in.

Notarized by Apple, so there's no security warning to work around. macOS confirms the first open, then two permissions let it see your library.

1

Install and open

Drag Library Cleanup into your Applications folder and open it. macOS asks once whether you're sure you downloaded it. Click Open.

2

Allow Photos

macOS asks for Photos access as soon as the app opens, before any scan. Click Allow so Library Cleanup can remove the photos you pick.

3

Grant Full Disk Access

System Settings ▸ Privacy & Security ▸ Full Disk Access ▸ add Library Cleanup. Without it, the app can't read your Photos library.

FAQ

The questions worth asking.

Does it also delete from iCloud?

If you use iCloud Photos, removals sync across your devices like any deletion you make in Photos. That's exactly why everything goes to Recently Deleted first: you get 30 days to change your mind, on every device.

Is it really free? What's the catch?

Really free. No trial, no locked features, no subscription. I built it for my own library and decided to share it. If it saves you time, a tip is appreciated, but that's the entire business model.

Is it signed and notarized?

Yes. It's signed with an Apple Developer ID and notarized by Apple, so macOS opens it without any security detour. You'll still see the standard “downloaded from the internet” confirmation the first time, the same one every app gets. Notarization means Apple scanned the app for malware; the public source is there so you can check what it actually does with your library.

Will it run on an Intel Mac?

Not yet. Current builds are Apple Silicon only (M1 and newer), on macOS 13 Ventura or later.

What does it never do?

It never uploads your photos, never edits or re-compresses your originals, and never deletes without your confirmation. The only network call it makes is an anonymous check to GitHub for a newer version. The source is public, so you don't have to take my word for it.
