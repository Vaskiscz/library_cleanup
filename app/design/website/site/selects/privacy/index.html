Selects · Privacy

Privacy policy

Last updated November 6, 2026

This policy explains how the Selects app for iPhone handles your data. Selects is a photo‑curation app: it groups your near‑identical photos into photoshoots, pre‑picks the best ones, and lets you clear the rest. It is developed by Václav Trnka (the “developer”, “I”), IČO 03940110, Tylova Čtvrť 378, 384 02 Lhenice, Czechia, who is responsible for the data described here.

The short version. Selects works on your iPhone. Your photos are analyzed on‑device and are never uploaded or sent anywhere. There is no account and no advertising.

The app does send anonymous usage statistics to help improve it. They describe how the app is used and, in counts and sizes, what your library holds, but never your photos themselves, and they are never tied to you. You can turn them off at any time in Settings. If you write feedback in the app, what you write is sent too.

Your photos stay on your iPhone

When you grant Photos access, Selects reads your library on your device to find photoshoots and score which shots are sharpest and most distinct. All of this analysis happens locally on your device.

Your photos and thumbnails never leave your device. What the anonymous statistics below do say about your library is limited to counts, sizes and the technical format of videos, never the photos themselves. Apart from those statistics, the one other thing that leaves the device is described under Place names below.

You can grant full or limited access, and change it at any time in the iOS Settings app. When you choose to remove photos, Selects hands them to iOS’s own delete, so they go to your Recently Deleted album and remain recoverable for 30 days. Nothing is ever removed without your confirmation.

Place names

To label a photoshoot with where it happened (for example, “Prague”), Selects asks Apple’s location services to turn a location into a place name. For this one lookup it sends only a coarse, rounded coordinate (about a kilometer, enough for a city name) to Apple; it never sends your photos or their exact locations, and nothing is sent to the developer.

Anonymous usage statistics

To understand how the app is used and where it can be improved, Selects sends anonymous usage statistics to PostHog, hosted in the European Union. They cover:

  • How the app is used: which screens and features are opened, and what happens in them, as fixed categories and counts (for example, how many photos were kept and cleared in a review, and the estimated space cleared).
  • What your library holds, in numbers: how many photos, videos, screenshots and other kinds of items it has, and how much space they take. Usually this is in ranges, at most once every 30 days. Once per install, it is the exact count and size of each list in Tidy up.
  • The technical format of videos you open or compress: resolution, frame rate, codec, whether it is HDR or slow motion, its length in ranges and its size rounded down to 10 MB, plus how a compression went, including error codes if it failed.
  • Taps that seem stuck: when the same spot is tapped repeatedly, the screen it happened on and the position of the taps, so unresponsive buttons can be found.
  • Survey answers: the app may occasionally ask a short question. Answering is optional, and answers are sent the same anonymous way.

They never include your photos, filenames, the dates or places your photos were taken, album names, or any identifier of a photo. Selects does not build advertising profiles, use the advertising identifier (IDFA), track you across other apps or websites, or record your screen. A random identifier is created when you install the app, so unique installs can be counted rather than one person counted many times. It is not your device's own identifier, it is not linked to you, and deleting the app discards it. PostHog sees your IP address as any connection over the internet does. It is used once to look up the country you are in, and then discarded: the country is kept, the IP address is not, and nothing finer than the country (no city or region) is recorded.

The legal basis is the developer’s legitimate interest in understanding and improving the app (GDPR Article 6(1)(f)), balanced by the statistics being anonymous and easy to switch off.

Your choice. Anonymous statistics are on by default. You can turn them off at any time in the app’s privacy settings, and the app works exactly the same either way.

Feedback you write

When the app asks how Selects is working for you, you can write a few lines about what could be better. If you send them, the text you wrote (up to 1,000 characters) goes to the developer through the same anonymous statistics. It carries no name or email address, so I can read it but cannot reply. If you want an answer, email selects@vaclavtrnka.cz instead, and please don’t put personal details in the feedback box.

This box is only offered while anonymous statistics are on. With them off, the app opens an email draft instead, and nothing is sent unless you send that email yourself.

Crash and error reports

Selects sends anonymous crash and error diagnostics (also through PostHog) so that bugs can be found and fixed. These reports are technical only, describing what failed and where, and are scrubbed of anything that could identify your photos. They follow the same on‑by‑default opt‑out as usage statistics above.

Tips

Selects is free, and tips are entirely optional. They unlock nothing in the app. If you choose to leave one, the payment is handled entirely by Apple’s In‑App Purchase. Apple processes the transaction; the developer never receives your name, card, or payment details. The app records only that a tip of a given size was made, as an anonymous event.

No account, no ads, no selling

Selects has no sign‑up and no user accounts. It shows no advertising, and your data is never sold, rented, or shared with data brokers.

Who your data reaches

Only two companies are involved, and neither receives your photos:

  • Apple: the App Store, In‑App Purchase, and the on‑device services the app relies on, under the Apple Privacy Policy.
  • PostHog: the EU‑hosted analytics that receive the anonymous events above, under the PostHog Privacy Policy.

Data retention

The developer keeps no personal data about you, because none is collected. The anonymous events are retained by PostHog under its standard retention settings. Turning off analytics in Settings stops any further events from being sent.

Children

Selects is not directed to children, and it does not knowingly collect data from children.

Your rights

Because the statistics Selects collects are anonymous and are not linked to your identity, the developer generally cannot single out, retrieve, or delete an individual person’s data. You remain in full control on the device: you can turn analytics off at any time in Settings, and revoke Photos access in the iOS Settings app. If you are in the EU, EEA, or UK and have questions about your rights under the GDPR, please get in touch. You can also complain to a data protection authority; in the Czech Republic that is the Office for Personal Data Protection (ÚOOÚ).

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the updated version will be posted here with a new date at the top.
